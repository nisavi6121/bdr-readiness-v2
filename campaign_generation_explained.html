2 · How many events each person gets

3 · Recent vs old (recency bias)

Senior real prospects and active individual contributors get steered toward the recent 2026 campaigns — concentrating fresh activity on the right people. This matters because the model rewards recency over ancient history.

4 · What kind of engagement (the status)

Each event's status fits the campaign type. "Responded" = a real human action; "Sent" = automated noise. Inflated people get "Sent" ~82% of the time.

5 · Shared-mailbox inflation

If a record sits on a shared inbox (info@), every one of its events is cloned onto all other records on that inbox. One open inflates everyone — the real "engagement smeared across duplicates" problem.

Why build it this way?

If engagement were sprinkled randomly, the model could "win" just by counting events — and the brief warns raw counts are misleading. By tying engagement to account quality and persona, concentrating recency on real prospects, and flooding 30% with automation, the data forces the model to separate real buying interest from automated busywork — the core challenge of the task.
