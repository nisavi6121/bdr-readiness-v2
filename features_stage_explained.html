Fairness mechanic: missing values are filled with the median of that person's own entity type (not the global mean), floor 0.30. In your data that's a lot — persona was blank for 248 leads (~41%) and 152 contacts (~38%). Non-Prospects map to a real 0.0, so they're never imputed.

Assembly — the keys that matter

The merge keys are the clever bit — they decide what's shared vs per-record:

engagement → scoring_person_id
A connected lead + contact share the same merged engagement (one human's activity).
account & profile → record_id
Each record keeps its own account & profile fit (lead may be L3, its contact C1).
So a pair has identical engagement but different fit — exactly what lets the later "promotion" step lift the contact using the lead's signal without losing its better firmographics. (People with no history get engagement = 0, never dropped.)

Why build it this way?

Every "Great"-rubric idea lives here: recency over depth (decay × multiplication), automation discounting (genuine-only + the 70% flag), no circular MQL dependence (segment = data source), and fairness to sparse records (within-type median + ZoomInfo fallback). Out comes features.csv, ready to normalize and blend.
