How the People Data Is Generated

generate_people() — casting 1,000 fake people, then dirtying the data like a real CRM

The job isn't to make tidy data. It's to make realistically messy data, so the scoring model has to prove it can cope with real-world mess.

Lead — 600

A business card in the fishbowl. You have their info, but they're not formally filed yet. Top of funnel.

Contact — 400

A person properly filed under a company in your address book. Further along the journey.

1 · The cast

Everyone gets a random first and last name from a big name bank. That's it — 1,000 blank people to start.

2 · Where do they work?

Each person is linked to one of the 200 companies — but 15% get no company at all, just like real CRMs full of floating, unattached records. That one gap weakens everything downstream.

4 · Their email — and the mess

A clean work email is built from name + company. Then reality is injected: some use free email (and the system misses a third of those), and duplicates are created on purpose in three flavors.

one email pasted onto another record

5 · Dates & compliance

Many records get a fake "imported on this day" date instead of when the person really entered — so timestamps can't be trusted. Then the "be careful / don't bother" flags get stamped on.

6 · The split — leads vs contacts

Now they're split, and each type gets different fields — including the real-world annoyance that the same score lives under different field names.

Leads get
  • 28% MQL + lead status
  • Marketo score (10% reset to 0)
  • re-MQL cycle count, DQ reasons
  • ZoomInfo enrichment
  • messy self-typed company name
Contacts get
  • 35% MQL
  • score under a different field name
  • thinner overall — fewer extras
7 · Conversion — linking lead to contact

200 leads "convert" into contacts, with pointers written both ways. But 20% of those links are broken — marked converted, yet the pointer is missing. A classic real bug: you know they converted, but not to what.

Why build it this way?

A clean, tidy fake dataset would make the scoring model look good for the wrong reasons. By baking in missing fields, junk personas, duplicate emails, untrustworthy dates, and broken links, the data forces the model to prove it can handle real mess — which is exactly what the assignment is testing.
