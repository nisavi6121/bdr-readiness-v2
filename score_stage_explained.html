How the Score Stage Works

03_score.py — three ingredients → one comparable 0-100 score

Make the three signals comparable, blend them with type-specific weights, and tag confidence on a separate axis.

Why only engagement? It's an unbounded sum ranging into the thousands (ceilings: Lead 1,452 · Contact 1,622). The other two are already calibrated — normalizing them again would distort a meaningful scale.
★ Why the 95th percentile, not the max
Normalize by MAX ✗
one outlier defines the ceiling → everyone squashed
Normalize by 95th-pct ✓
ignores the top-5% spikes → scores spread out
Cost of the 95th-pct approach: the top 5% all read 100, so you can't rank within the very hottest — fine, they're all "call now." In your data exactly 5.2% of each type saturates at 100.
2 Non-prospect override

Any non-prospect has account fit and profile fit forced to 0 — a competitor shouldn't display "great account fit," even though they're blocked anyway. Keeps the score internally honest.

Engagement is heavily zero-inflated (median 0.1) — ~51% of leads and ~47% of contacts have no genuine engagement. The model is correctly saying most people aren't ready now.
The final score is selective — median ~13 with a long tail to 96. Most sit low, a select few score high: exactly the "top 500 to call" shape, not a list where everyone looks hot.
The score stage is all about robust, fair comparison: 95th-pct normalization within type (outliers can't squash the field, leads vs contacts judged separately), only the wild signal normalized, differentiated weights, non-prospect zeroing for honesty, stored weights for transparency, and confidence as an independent axis.
